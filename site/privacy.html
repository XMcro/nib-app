Privacy Policy

Nib for iPhone, iPad and Mac. Last updated 13 November 2026.

Nib is a writing app that keeps your writing on your device. It has no accounts, no advertising and no tracking of any kind, and it sends usage counts only if you say yes.

Without Nib Plus

Everything you write, and its version history, is stored only on your device. None of your writing is sent to us or to anyone else unless you turn on sync with Nib Plus or share a draft (see below). Nib sends us nothing else, except a code for coins if you enter one, usage counts if you say yes, and any feedback you choose to send, as described below. A backup copy of your library is also kept in the app’s own files on your device, where it is included in your device backups if you use them: iCloud Backup on iPhone and iPad, or Time Machine on a Mac.

Your appearance and sort preferences stay on your device too, with or without Nib Plus. So do your goals: the daily word goal and its progress, the words you write each day for the writing calendar, and any sprint in progress. The style check reads your writing on your device and sends it nowhere.

From Nib 1.10.09, the stamps you collect, the coins you earn and spend on them, the days you opened a pack or collected coins, the fingerprints of codes for coins you have entered (see below), and when you bought days of Nib Plus with coins are kept in your iCloud account, in Apple’s iCloud key-value storage, so they’re the same on every iPhone, iPad and Mac signed in to your Apple Account. They’re stored by Apple under your account, and this copy is never sent to us. Your writing is never kept there. With iCloud turned off, they stay on your device.

If you enter a code for coins, Nib sends our server the code’s fingerprint (a one-way hash of it, rather than the code) and a random number for that attempt, so the code’s uses can be counted. The server keeps the fingerprint, the number, when it was used and how many coins it gave. Its answer is signed, so Nib can tell the coins came from our server. To limit how many codes one person can use in a day, it also counts how many have been used from your IP address (for an IPv6 address, its network) and from the wider network it belongs to, each under an identifier derived from it with a secret key, never the address itself, deleted at the end of the day (UTC). Like any web service, it briefly handles your device’s IP address to answer, and does not store it.

If you add a picture from a link, Nib fetches that picture once, straight from your device, from the address you gave. The site at that address sees your device’s IP address and that the picture was asked for, as it would if you opened the address in a browser. Nothing about you or your writing is sent with the request, and nothing is sent to us. The picture is then kept in your document like any other, and Nib does not contact that address again.

Lock Nib uses Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode through your device. Nib is told only whether it was you. It never sees your face, fingerprint or passcode.

Quick capture keeps what you send to Nib from the share sheet or Shortcuts in a folder on your device that only Nib can read, until Nib next opens and files it as a note.

Exports and backups you make are files you choose where to put. Nib does not see them.

Importing reads the files you choose on your device, and sends nothing of them anywhere. If a backup you import needs a password, as ColorNote’s can, Nib uses it on your device to open the file, and doesn’t keep it.

With Nib Plus sync

If you subscribe to Nib Plus and turn on sync, your writing and its history, and from Nib 1.10.09 the stamps you’ve collected, are sent to Nib’s sync server so your other devices can fetch them. Before anything leaves your device, it is encrypted with a key derived from your sync code. The code never leaves your devices. The server stores only the encrypted copies, and neither we nor anyone else can read them.

A document’s own goal, and the links you’ve made to share it (see below), travel with the document, encrypted in the same way, so each of your devices can see and manage them.

With Nib Plus, Nib keeps a picture’s full-quality original as a file on your device, beside the smaller copy in the document. If sync is on, each original is sent to the sync server too, encrypted on your device with the same key as your writing, under a name the server can’t match to a document. A device fetches one the first time it shows or exports the document it belongs to.

A file you attach to a note or a page, also with Nib Plus, is kept and sent in the same way. Its name stays in the document, encrypted with it, and a device fetches the file when you open it. Together, a library’s originals and attached files can take up to 2 GB on the server, and each can be up to 25 MB.

A locked document keeps no originals, on your devices or on the server, and can’t hold attached files. A document you keep on one device only has its originals and attached files taken off the server. An original or an attached file that no document uses any more, in its text or its history, is deleted from your device a day or two later, when you next open Nib, and from the server at the next sync.

The inbox. With sync on, you can turn on an inbox in Settings → Sync: an address that other apps and services can send notes to. This is the one case where writing reaches our server before it is encrypted, because the service that sends a note can’t encrypt it for you. The server encrypts each note the moment it arrives, with a key that only your devices can open, and keeps nothing of it that it can read, in its storage or its logs. It holds the encrypted note until one of your devices next syncs and takes it in, then deletes it. A note that no device takes in is deleted after 30 days. An inbox holds at most 200 notes waiting, and takes at most 200 in a day. See how to send to it.

The address ends in a secret. Anyone who has the address can send notes to your inbox, though they can’t read anything of yours, so keep it as you would a password. “New address” in Settings → Sync replaces it at once, and “Turn off the inbox” removes it and deletes any notes still waiting. The server keeps only a one-way hash of the secret. Nib Web Clipper sends the secret in a header, apart from the address. A service that posts to the whole address sends it in the address, where Cloudflare, which runs our server, may record it with the other addresses it serves. The key that opens your notes is kept on the server too, encrypted with the key from your sync code, so your devices can fetch it and the server can’t read it. The app or service you send from sees whatever you send through it, under its own privacy policy.

Nib Web Clipper. Nib’s extension for Chrome, Edge and Firefox saves web pages to your inbox from your computer’s browser. It reads a page only when you click its button on that page, to show you what there is to keep, and it reads nothing of the other pages you visit. What you choose to keep, with the page’s title and address and any note you add, is encrypted in your browser with a key that only your devices can open, and sent to our server and nowhere else. The server holds it as it holds any note in the inbox, and at no point can it read it. The extension keeps one thing, in your browser on that computer: the clipper code you paste into it from Settings → Sync, which is your inbox’s address with a key that can encrypt for your devices and can’t open anything. Anyone who has the code can send to your inbox, though they can’t read anything of yours, so keep it as you would the address. Removing the extension deletes the code. A clip can be up to 1 MB once encrypted, and an inbox holds at most 20 MB waiting.

What the server can see:

  • a random identifier for your library, derived from your code;
  • how many encrypted records it holds, roughly how large they are (each is padded, so its exact size is hidden), and when they changed;
  • how many full-quality pictures and attached files it holds, roughly how large each is (padded in the same way), and when each arrived;
  • whether you have an inbox, and if so: each note sent to it, for the moment it takes to encrypt it (a clip from Nib Web Clipper arrives already encrypted, and is never readable); how many are waiting, how large each is encrypted, and when it arrived; and how many it has taken that day;
  • a public key made from your sync code, which the server uses to check that sync requests come from your devices, and whether you have asked it to accept only those (Require signed sync). It cannot be used to read your writing;
  • your App Store subscription transaction, signed by Apple, which we check to confirm your subscription is active. It includes the product, its expiry date and a transaction number. It does not include your name, email address or Apple Account details;
  • an identifier derived from that subscription, kept with your library, so that one subscription can sync up to five libraries. A library that hasn’t been renewed for 60 days stops counting toward the five.

If Apple tells us that a purchase was refunded or revoked, through the App Store’s server notifications, we record that against the same identifier, and sync and drafts shared with Nib Plus stop until a later purchase. Apple’s notice includes the same transaction details as above, and nothing more about you.

Like any web service, the server briefly handles your device’s IP address to answer its requests. We do not store it or use it for anything else. The server runs on Cloudflare, which processes that traffic on our behalf.

A library started from 1 November 2026, including one made when you change your sync code, is stored in Cloudflare’s European Union jurisdiction. One started before then stays where it was first stored. Full-quality pictures and attached files are stored in Cloudflare’s European Union jurisdiction whenever the library was started.

From Nib 1.10.08, past versions of a document are kept on the server for 90 days. After that, Nib asks the server to delete their encrypted contents, so a device that starts syncing receives at most 90 days of history. Each of your devices keeps its own history in full.

Because only you hold the key, we cannot recover your writing if you lose your sync code. Your devices keep their own copies regardless.

Sharing a draft

You can share a draft of a document by link. Without Nib Plus, you can share one document at a time, for 7 days, and readers can only read it. With Nib Plus, a link can last 7, 30 or 90 days and have a password, readers can leave notes, and you can share more than one document at once. A link shared with Nib Plus can also be one to be read once. Either way, Nib makes a copy of the document as it is at that moment and encrypts it on your device with a new key made just for that link. The key is part of the link, after the “#”, the part that browsers never send to a server. The server stores only the encrypted copy, and neither we nor anyone else without the link can read it.

Anyone you give the link to can read the draft in a web browser, and on a link shared with Nib Plus, leave you a note on it, with a name if they like. Notes are encrypted on the reader’s device with the same key before they are sent. Only you see them; other readers don’t.

A link to be read once. Opening the link shows the reader a page that says what it is, and waits. When they choose to open the draft, the server hands over the encrypted copy and deletes it in the same step, and the link stops working for everyone. You are told that it has been read, and the reader is told beforehand that you will be. The server keeps nothing about who read it or when.

How many times a link is opened. For any other link shared with Nib Plus, the server counts how many times the link has been opened, once a day for each network it’s opened from, so opening it again from the same place on the same day isn’t counted twice, and Share a draft shows you the number. To count each network once, the server keeps a hash of the network, keyed with a secret, until the end of that day, and nothing else about who opened the link or when. The reader’s page says that the link is counted. Opening the link yourself in a browser counts, and so can an app that loads the page to show a preview of it. Links shared without Nib Plus are not counted.

What the server can see about a shared draft:

  • a random identifier for the link;
  • how large the encrypted copy is, when it was shared, and when it expires;
  • how many notes it has, how large they are, and when they arrived;
  • for a draft shared with Nib Plus, how many times its link has been opened, or, for one to be read once, whether it has been read;
  • for a draft shared with Nib Plus, your App Store subscription transaction, as for sync, which we check to confirm your subscription is active. Shares made under the same subscription are filed together, so we can count them and limit how many you have at once;
  • for a draft shared without Nib Plus, a count of how many free links have been started from your IP address that day (for an IPv6 address, its network: the first half of the address), and another for the wider network it belongs to, which a home, office or school may share (the first three numbers of an IPv4 address, or the first three groups of an IPv6 one). Each count is kept under an identifier derived from the address or network with a secret key, never the address itself, and is deleted at the end of the day (UTC), along with everything that held it. They are used only to limit how many free links one address, or one network, can make. We also count how many free links are started each day in all, with no address at all;
  • for a note left on a draft, the same two counts of how many notes have come from the reader’s IP address (or IPv6 network), and from its wider network, for that draft that day, kept and deleted the same way, and used only to limit how many notes one reader, or one network, can leave.

As with sync, the server briefly handles the IP address of your device and of each reader’s browser to answer their requests, and does not store them, apart from those daily counts. The reader’s page loads nothing from anywhere else and sets no cookies.

Ending a share. “Stop sharing” deletes the encrypted copy, its notes and its count from the server at once. Otherwise they are deleted automatically when the link expires. A record that the link was used is kept, with nothing of its contents, so that no one else can reuse it. Deleting the document for good in Nib stops sharing it too, when your device is online. If your subscription lapses, drafts shared with Nib Plus stop opening after a short grace period, and are deleted when they expire.

Reports. Each shared draft has a “Report this page” link, which writes an email to us that includes the full link. If the reader sends it, we can open the draft to review it. We may turn off any shared draft that breaks the law or the App Store’s rules.

Usage counts, if you say yes

From Nib 1.10.10, Nib asks once whether it may send usage counts. Nothing is counted or sent unless you say yes, and you can change your answer at any time in Settings.

If you say yes, Nib keeps a daily total on your device of which features were used, such as how many times Export was used. Once a day is over, it sends that day’s totals with the date, the kind of device (iPhone, iPad or Mac) and Nib’s version. No account, name, device identifier or other ID goes with them, so the server can’t tell who sent them, or that two days’ totals came from the same device. They never include anything you’ve written, or any title. The server adds them to its running totals and keeps nothing else of the request.

To limit how many can be sent, the server counts them per network for the day, by a hash keyed with a secret, and forgets that at the end of the day.

Sending feedback

From Nib 1.10.10, you can send us feedback in Settings. Your message goes to Nib’s server, which keeps it, with the kind of device (iPhone, iPad or Mac), Nib’s version and the day it arrived, until we delete it. It is stored in Cloudflare’s European Union jurisdiction. Unlike your synced writing, it isn’t encrypted so that only you can read it, because we need to read it. Nib adds no name, email address or identifier. Anything you choose to write in it, such as an address to reply to, reaches us. We use these messages to decide what to build and fix, and to answer you if you ask us to. If you’d like a message removed, contact us and tell us what it said.

When a message arrives, we get an alert on our phone in the Bark app, sent through Bark’s server and Apple’s push service. The alert is encrypted so that neither can read it, says only that a message is waiting, and has none of your words in it.

To limit how many can be sent, the server counts them per network for the day, by a hash keyed with a secret, and forgets that at the end of the day.

So that the feedback offer appears once, and its two free weeks of Nib Plus’s features are given once, Nib keeps a small marker in your device’s Keychain. It stays there after Nib is deleted, holds nothing else, and never leaves your device.

Deleting your data

In Settings → Sync, “Delete everything on the server” removes your library, its full-quality pictures and attached files, and its inbox with any notes waiting, from the server immediately. Turning off sync, or cancelling Nib Plus, leaves your writing on your devices. If your subscription lapses, sync stops after a short grace period, and if it isn’t renewed, your library is deleted from the server 12 months after that, and its full-quality pictures and attached files with it. Once a library is deleted, the server keeps only a mark that one was there, so that no new library is ever made at its old address. Cloudflare, which runs our server, keeps 30 days of the history of its database, so for 30 days after something in it is deleted, it could still be restored, encrypted as it was. We would only do that to recover from a failure.

Payments

Nib Plus is sold through Apple’s App Store. Apple handles payment and billing; we never see your payment details. Apple’s own privacy policy covers that part.

Children

Nib collects no personal information from anyone, including children. Notes left on a shared draft can include a name the reader chooses to give; they are encrypted, and we cannot read them. A feedback message holds only what its writer chooses to put in it.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the date above changes with it, and anything that affects what leaves your device will be said in the app first. Questions can go through the support link on Nib’s App Store page. Reports about shared drafts go to support@nibprivacy.com.
